The Approach

Content isn't just "Help"—it's a product.

I’ve spent the last 9 years figuring out how to make technical info scale. To me, content isn't just something you ship at the end of a project; it's a core part of the user experience. If a customer can't find the answer they need, the product has failed them.

I focus on building "architectures of participation"—systems where engineering, product, and support teams can actually work together instead of in silos. Whether it's setting up a CCMS for GenAI or moving a team to a "Docs-as-Code" workflow, I build for longevity and speed.

Key Projects

Making an Impact

Architecture

Moving 20k topics to the Cloud

NETGEAR

The Problem: Our documentation was stuck in legacy formats that were slow to update and basically invisible to modern tools like AI chatbots.

The Fix: I led the migration of over 20,000 topics into a SaaS CCMS (Heretto). This turned our static docs into a "Content-as-a-Service" model that feeds everything from support tickets to in-product guides.

The Result: We cut down costs and finally made our content accessible via API, which is a huge win for our future AI and search goals.

Operations

Fixing the "Visibility" Problem

NETGEAR

The Problem: With a global team, it was hard to see who was working on what, where the bottlenecks were, or how much we could actually handle in a quarter.

The Fix: I built out a custom Jira ecosystem specifically for our content workflows. It wasn't just about tickets; it was about capturing data on how we actually work.

The Result: We now have real-time data on our velocity. This made capacity planning way more accurate and kept us running smoothly even during tough hiring periods.

Efficiency

From 3 weeks to 1 day

NETGEAR

The Problem: Our old publication cycle took three weeks. If there was a critical security update or a product launch, we just couldn't move fast enough.

The Fix: I reengineered our process into a "Continuous Delivery" model. We moved away from rigid cycles and gave the team the tools to own their own releases.

The Result: We can now ship critical info in a single day. It turned a massive bottleneck into a streamlined, agile process.
